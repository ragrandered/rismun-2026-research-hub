01 · Agenda overview

Why countries are competing for AI infrastructure

AI is software that finds patterns and makes predictions. To run it at scale, countries need scarce physical inputs: advanced chips, data centres, electricity, networks, water, minerals and skilled people. Control of these inputs increasingly shapes economic and strategic power.

What is it?

Compute = digital work capacity

Compute is the processing power used to train or run AI. A GPU cluster can complete billions of calculations each second.

Simple example: compute is the engine power behind an AI assistant.

Why is it important?

Geopolitics = power across borders

Geopolitics asks how geography, resources and trade affect power. AI makes chip factories, cables and power grids strategic assets.

MUN use: connect national security with fair access and development.

Why Indonesia?

The AI boom changes demand

Generative AI has sharply increased demand for high-end chips and reliable electricity. Building capacity can take years and cost billions.

Indonesia: can shape regional standards and supply links.

02 · Country profile

Indonesia: a pivotal ASEAN bridge

Debate-ready position

  • World’s fourth-largest population; a major digital market and ASEAN leader.
  • Nickel, copper and other resources create leverage in clean-tech and infrastructure supply chains.
  • Seeks digital sovereignty while welcoming investment, technology transfer and regional cooperation.
  • Can advocate affordable, sustainable compute—not a closed technology bloc.

Core line

“AI infrastructure must support inclusive development, resilient supply chains and a just energy transition.”

Foreign policy

Indonesia traditionally follows an independent and active foreign policy. In committee, it can avoid binary US–China framing and build issue-based coalitions.

Energy and data centres

New facilities need dependable power, cooling and fibre. A credible Indonesian proposal pairs growth with renewable procurement, geothermal and efficiency rules.

Likely pressure point

Delegates may question coal dependence and mining impacts. Answer with transition finance, standards, traceability, community safeguards and diversified generation.

03–06 · Government, economy & policy

How Indonesia makes decisions

Constitutional system

Indonesia is a presidential republic. The President leads the executive branch; the DPR is the elected national legislature; courts interpret law. Ministries convert national priorities into programmes.

Debate use: speak as a national government: propose cooperation that respects domestic law and national planning.

Free and active diplomacy

Bebas aktif means Indonesia does not automatically join a great-power camp, but actively seeks peaceful, fair solutions. ASEAN is its central regional platform.

Debate use: build bridges between security-minded and development-minded blocs.

Economic reality

Indonesia’s 2024 GDP was about US$1.40 trillion, with 5.0% annual growth; population was about 283.5 million. Large demand is an advantage, but affordability and equal access remain central.

03–09 · The technology stack

Infrastructure, chips, compute, data centres & energy

Semiconductors

Semiconductors are materials whose electricity flow can be controlled. Chips made from them are the brains of phones, cars and AI servers.

  • CPU: general-purpose manager
  • GPU: parallel-math specialist
  • NPU/TPU: AI-focused accelerators

How chips are made

Design → wafer fabrication → lithography → packaging → testing. No country controls every stage: design, tools and fabrication are globally distributed.

Debate hook: resilience means diverse suppliers, not simply domestic production.

Data centres

Data centres house servers, storage and network equipment. AI centres need unusually dense power, advanced cooling and secure fibre connections.

Environmental question: who pays for grid upgrades and water impacts?

Cloud computing

Cloud providers rent computing over the internet. This lowers entry cost, but concentration can create dependency and data-governance concerns.

Energy

AI needs electricity every moment it runs. Solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, nuclear, gas and coal have different reliability, cost and emissions trade-offs.

Indonesia: geothermal is a powerful clean-energy narrative.

Critical minerals

Nickel, copper, cobalt, lithium, graphite and rare earths support equipment, grids, batteries and electronics. Extraction must be paired with transparent, sustainable standards.

12 · Indonesia’s proposals

Build a coalition around access, resilience and sustainability

ASEAN Compute Commons

Shared regional compute credits for universities, public-interest research and start-ups.

  • Benefit: reduces entry barriers
  • Funding: blended public, development-bank and private funds
  • Challenge: governance and fair allocation

Green Data Centre Compact

Regional standards for renewable procurement, efficiency, water reporting and grid contributions.

  • Benefit: credible growth with safeguards
  • Implementation: voluntary first, reporting baseline, then incentives

Responsible Minerals Partnership

Traceability, cleaner processing, local skills and community protections across critical-mineral supply chains.

  • Benefit: more value at home and trusted supply
  • Challenge: cost and verification capacity

AI Infrastructure Fund

International fund for grids, cables, skills, cybersecurity and sustainable facilities in developing states.

Technology Transfer Compact

Pair investment incentives with training, research partnerships and local supplier development—not forced transfers.

Cyber & Sovereignty Protocol

Common minimum security, interoperable data rules and incident response channels that protect national choice.

13–14 · MUN guide

Turn research into persuasive diplomacy

Speech blueprint

  1. Hook: name the shared problem.
  2. Position: state Indonesia’s principle.
  3. Proof: give one fact or practical example.
  4. Proposal: offer a workable action.
  5. Invite: name partners you want to work with.

Opening line: “Honourable Chair, Indonesia believes that AI infrastructure must not become a new divide between those who control computation and those who merely consume it.”

Procedure quick answers

Be concise, respectful and solution-focused. Refer to the agenda, listen carefully and move debate forward.
Name a narrow sub-topic, useful speaker time and a reason it advances debate.
Start with shared interests, offer language others can own, and record agreements in a working paper.
Clear actions, responsible actors, funding, timelines, reporting and safeguards.
